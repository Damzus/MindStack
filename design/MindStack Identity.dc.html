3Motion marks — four animated behaviours

All four are live and looping here so you can judge them; in production each fires once, in the place named on the card. A logo that loops forever on a marketing page reads as a loading state. Every one degrades to its final frame under prefers-reduced-motion, and each is built from the same geometry as 2a and 2c, so nothing new has to be drawn.

3aAssemble — the plane draws itself
MindStack
ORDER: EDGE → NODES → CORE → LAYER
1100ms TOTAL
Use on first page load only, once per session. The strongest storytelling of the four: structure first, then the parts, then the layer underneath.
3bFold — the top plane folds onto the base
MindStack
COMPRESS → SETTLE → SEAM LIGHTS
620ms, ONE OVERSHOOT
Use on nav hover and page transitions. Short, physical, and it acts out the name. The one I'd put in the header.
3cRelay — seam nodes carry the signal
mindstack
LEFT → RIGHT RELAY, 1800ms LOOP
THE ONE THAT MAY LOOP
Use as the loading and deploying state — in the app, on form submit, wherever something is in flight. Reads as data moving between layers.
3dOrbit — lattice turns around a fixed core
MindStack
7s ROTATION · CORE BEATS AT 2.4s
AMBIENT, VERY SLOW
Use large only — a footer or a full-screen menu. Charming at 120px, illegible in a 20px header, and the rotation breaks the isometric read.

2Fold × Kernel hybrids

Three ways to marry 1e's two planes with 1d's dot grid. The shared logic: the plane is the structure MindStack builds, the nodes are the systems it connects, and the seam is where the two layers meet. All built from circles and four-point polygons, so they hold at 16px and in one colour.

2aNode Fold — outlined plane, solid core, folded layer
MindStack
reversed
How it combines them — the Fold silhouette, with Kernel's node dots pinned to the plane's three visible vertices and its solid core at the centre. The lower plane is a ghost, so the mark reads as one object rather than two.
Best at — 24px and up; the strongest and most distinctive of the three.
Watch — at 16px the vertex dots merge into the outline, so the favicon drops to two solid planes (shown right).
2bLattice — the plane drawn entirely from nodes
MindStack
reversed
How it combines them — Kernel's grid re-plotted onto Fold's isometric geometry: the dots trace the plane's edge rather than sitting in a square, with the solid core at the centre and the second plane implied beneath.
Best at — large sizes and animation; the dots are a natural staggered build-on.
Watch — the busiest of the three. Needs the simplified favicon below 24px.
2cSeam — two solid planes, nodes in the join
mindstack
reversed
How it combines them — Fold reduced to two solid planes, with Kernel's nodes living in the seam: the systems being connected sit between the layers.
Best at — every size. Simplest silhouette, survives 16px unchanged, one-colour safe.
Watch — closest to Fold as-was; the Kernel contribution is subtle.

1MindStack — five logo & identity directions

Working assumption (unconfirmed): MindStack is a platform for building and running AI agents on a company's own knowledge and systems. Every mark below is built from plain geometry so it survives at 16px and in one colour. Nothing here is illustrative — I can't generate imagery.

1aLedger — stacked layers, weighted
MindStack
reversed
Idea — three bars of decreasing width: raw data, reasoning, answer. The stack read literally, narrowing to a point.
Strength — unmistakable at favicon size; the accent bar gives a natural loading/progress animation.
Risk — bar stacks are common in data branding.
1bAperture — editorial, nested squares
MindStack
reversed
Idea — a frame with a solid core: the model inside the guardrails. Serif wordmark buys credibility with non-technical buyers.
Strength — reads as institution, not startup. Very few AI companies use a serif.
Risk — the square-in-square is abstract; it needs the wordmark to mean anything.
1cOffset M — monogram from four bars
MindStack
reversed
Idea — an M drawn as four columns of different heights. Monogram and bar chart at once; the last column carries the accent.
Strength — owns the letter M without a literal letterform. Columns animate well on scroll.
Risk — the M is not obvious until pointed out.
1dKernel — technical, dot grid
mindstack
reversed
Idea — eight nodes around one solid centre: scattered context, one place it resolves.
Strength — speaks fluently to developers; lowercase mono wordmark signals infra.
Risk — dots fill in at 16px; skews too dev for a CIO audience.
1eFold — two planes, one seam
MindStack
reversed
Idea — two isometric planes stacked with a visible seam: your systems and the intelligence layer above them.
Strength — most distinctive silhouette of the five; the seam is a strong hover/scroll device.
Risk — isometric planes are heavily used by cloud and database brands.
