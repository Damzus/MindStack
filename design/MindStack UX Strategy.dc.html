MindStack SITEMAP & UX STRATEGY · v1 · DRAFT

Information architecture and conversion strategy for the MindStack website

A ten-page site built around one conversion action — talk to sales — for a firm that designs and builds websites, applications and the cloud infrastructure that runs them.

01

What we know

CONFIRMED BY YOU
  • MindStack develops websites and applications for businesses, and supports cloud infrastructure.
  • Buyers span engineering leadership (CTO, VP Eng), business ops and finance leaders, and individual developers.
  • Primary conversion action is talk to sales.
  • Motion should be a showpiece — the site itself is proof of craft.
  • All ten pages are in scope.
MY ASSUMPTIONS — CORRECT THESE
  • You sell projects and retainers, not seat-based software. Pricing is therefore engagement models, not a price table.
  • Deal sizes justify a sales conversation, so there is no self-serve path.
  • You have delivered work you're allowed to name. Case studies depend on this.
  • The developer audience matters for hiring and credibility rather than purchase.
  • You want inbound leads from search and referral, not paid acquisition funnels.
DELIBERATELY EMPTY

No client names, logos, testimonials, metrics, team members, certifications or partnerships appear anywhere in this plan. Every such slot is a labelled placeholder. Fabricated proof is the fastest way to lose a technical buyer.

02

The strategic problem

Every agency site says the same three things: we listen, we're technical, we deliver. Buyers have stopped reading them. The differentiator available to a firm that builds and then runs software is continuity — the same team that ships the app owns the infrastructure it runs on, so nobody is handed a repository and left to operate it.

The narrative the site should carry

Build it properly, then keep it running. Design, engineering and infrastructure from one team, with the operational record to prove it.

What the site must prove, not claim

That the craft is real. The site's own performance, motion and detail are the primary evidence — which is why the motion system is a strategy item, not decoration.

05

Three journeys

One per buyer you named. Each ends at the same place by a different road.

VP Engineering · replatforming under a deadline
Arrives from a peer referral. Sceptical, technical, time-poor. Evaluates craft before claims.
Home→ Work→ Case detail→ How we build→ Engagement→ Contact

Design implication: Work must be reachable in one click from the hero, and case studies must survive technical reading. This is the journey the site should be optimised for.

Head of Operations · a manual process is breaking
Arrives from search with a problem, not a spec. Needs to see their own situation described.
Insight post→ Solutions→ Case detail→ Engagement→ Contact

Design implication: Solutions pages carry no jargon, and Engagement must answer "roughly what does this cost" before they'll make contact.

Developer · evaluating MindStack as a place to work or partner
Not a buyer. Influences buyers, and becomes the team that wins the work.
Insights→ How we build→ Careers→ Apply

Design implication: this audience gets depth, not a second CTA. Serve them through Insights and How we build; don't distort the commercial pages for them.

06

Motion as evidence

You asked for showpiece motion. Here is where it earns its place, and where it must stay out of the way.

Showpiece moments — three, no more
  • Hero: a working interface assembling itself, once, on load.
  • How we build: a scroll-driven walk through the architecture.
  • Case study opening: the before state resolving into the after.
Everywhere else — quiet system
  • Reveals: 12–16px rise, 400ms, one easing curve, staggered 60ms.
  • Hover: 120ms, state change only, never scale-and-shadow.
  • Nothing animates on the Contact page or in forms.
  • prefers-reduced-motion replaces every reveal with an instant state and freezes the hero on its final frame.
